# Hidden boundary tax

**Tier:** interpretive, with checkable instances.
**Origin:** coined in field notes, September 2026.

## The cost

The unpriced cost empaths pay to integrate: set boundaries, run the filter. Three layers, priced:

- **Material.** Lost gigs, lost income, lost opportunity. The boundary has a price tag and the empath pays it.
- **Social.** Read as cold or difficult. The virtue armor punishes the empath who stops over-giving: the same people who benefited from the boundlessness recast the boundary as a character flaw.
- **Internal.** Guilt, hypersensitivity backlash. The boundary hurts the setter. The attunement that makes the empath good at reading rooms makes them bad at tolerating the discomfort of holding a line.

## The structural read

The tax isn't a side effect. It's the enforcement mechanism. Systems that benefit from over-giving punish boundaries, which is why the pattern repeats across generations. The empath's double bind, stay open and get extracted, set boundaries and get taxed, is structural, not a character flaw. Telling empaths to just set boundaries without pricing the tax is telling someone to walk through a toll booth and pretending the toll isn't there.

## The relation

This is the cost side of the extraction map's raw materials. Over-giving is one of the four things extraction systems run on; the tax is what the system charges for stopping the flow.
