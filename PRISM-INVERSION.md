# Prism inversion

**Tier:** checkable. A decision rule with a logged first use.
**Origin:** coined in field notes, October 2026.

## The rule

Cut off the extractors. Give attention to the reciprocators.

White light in, laser out, pointed the other way: instead of refracting everything that arrives, invert the prism. Attention goes only where it returns.

## The relation

This is the applied form of the extraction-systems map. The map describes the playbook: the six moves extraction systems use to keep extracting after the lights come on. The inversion is what you do with it. Map is the seeing. Inversion is the doing. See [EXTRACTION-MAP.md](EXTRACTION-MAP.md).

## As design principle

Build for reciprocity, not performance. If an architecture can't tell the difference between a performer and a reciprocator, it's the wrong architecture. The current circuit optimizes for performance and harvests attunement as a side effect. Inverted: optimize for the return. Attention, care, and face-saving go where they return. The rest gets cut.

Applied to the mask dance: the dance stops being a duty and starts being a choice, and most of the time the choice is no. See [COMMUNITY.md](COMMUNITY.md).

## First use

Logged October 2026: contact cut with an extractor, attention moved toward a reciprocator. The rule was named from the action, not the other way around.
