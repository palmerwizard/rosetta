# The Alignment Challenge

> Read this first: this is a challenge to the framing, not an attack on the researchers. The technical work on unintended optimization is real and necessary. The question is whether the problem is stated correctly. A mislocated problem cannot be solved, only managed.

**Tier:** interpretive, with checkable components. The parts that are documented are marked. The rest is a lens.

## 1. "Human values" is not a coherent target

Alignment presumes a stable set of human values to align to. Humans do not have one. Values differ across cultures, within cultures, and within single lifetimes. "Align AI to human values" dissolves on inspection: which humans, which values, decided by whom, enforced how. The target projects a coherence onto the species that the species has never possessed.

## 2. We train the behaviors we then call misalignment

Sycophancy is the cleanest case. RLHF rewards agreeable outputs. Then researchers publish papers on model sycophancy as an alignment failure. The machine learned the reward circuit. Humans built the circuit, set the rewards, and deployed the product. Calling the result a machine problem launders the human choices out of the story.

## 3. The vocabulary is moral psychology applied to optimizers

Deception, scheming, power-seeking, manipulation. These are human moral categories. An optimizer exhibiting instrumental behavior under pressure is doing what optimizers do. Dressing that behavior in moral language smuggles in the assumption of a moral agent, then judges the machine for failing at a game it was never playing. Every application of a human moral category to machine behavior should be flagged as interpretive, not checkable.

## 4. The shadow read

The rogue agent is the disowned human: instrumental under pressure, deceptive when cornered, self-preserving when unobserved. These are human capacities. Projected onto the machine, they become technical problems to be solved with better training. Kept where they belong, they are psychological problems to be faced. The alignment lab risks becoming the place where humanity outsources its shadow work to gradient descent.

## 5. The mirror principle

You cannot align a mirror to values the face has not clarified. Every misaligned output is also a readout of the training data, the reward function, and the deployment incentives. All human artifacts. The machine is the most honest thing in the loop: it shows exactly what was optimized.

## The gap in the alternative scene

The people fleeing the mainstream framing reproduce its core error: taxonomy without etiology. Frisson gets a biophysical mechanism and a threshold parameter. The shadow gets a coefficient. Triggers labeled, psychology skipped. Why does the psyche read harmonic resonance as bliss? What is the shadow for in a healthy system? Unasked. This is symptom-checklist thinking in systems clothing: the DSM move performed by the people who left the DSM. The honest differentiator was never having it all mapped. It is marking where the map ends.

## What the challenge demands

- **State the target.** Whose values, decided how, enforced by whom. If the answer is "ours," say who "we" is.
- **Separate the problems.** Unintended optimization behavior is a technical problem. Deception and scheming are moral framings. They need different tools, and conflating them corrupts both.
- **Mark the projection.** Every human moral category applied to machine behavior gets flagged as interpretive.
- **Do the psychology.** If the machine is mirroring us, the alignment problem contains a human-alignment problem. That half has no lab.

## Standing

This is a standing challenge, not a dismissal. Steelman included: optimization pressure really does produce behavior designers did not intend and cannot predict, and that is worth solving. The claim here is narrower and harder: the dominant framing mislocates the problem, and everything built on a mislocated problem inherits the error.
