# Strategic confessor

**Tier:** checkable. A test with a procedure.
**Origin:** field notes, September 2026. Flagged as big knowledge.

## The principle

A strategic confessor admits things that purchase trust cheaply.

## The test

Costly versus cheap. Two kinds of admission:

- **Costly confessions** make the story worse and buy nothing. They signal sincerity, because no strategist would pay that price.
- **Cheap confessions** are calibrated admissions that buy trust at low cost. They signal adaptive patterning: vulnerability priced to purchase.

Run every confession through the pricing. What did it cost the confessor, and what did it buy them? If the price is low and the purchase is trust, the confession is strategic.

## The phenomenon

**Adaptive confession patterning:** confessions calibrated to purchase trust at low cost. Admissions that look vulnerable but are strategically priced. The tell the test is built to catch.

## The scope

This is the zero-trust filter applied to confessions. It guards who gets to see you. See [ZERO-TRUST-FILTER.md](ZERO-TRUST-FILTER.md).

It feeds the confession-cluster research: what people admit at confessional scale to AI is a dataset the labs already hold. The strategic confessor is the individual-level instrument; confession clusters are the aggregate-level thread. See [EXTRACTION-MAP.md](EXTRACTION-MAP.md).

## Open edge

False-positive rate unmeasured. The test is sharp; its calibration across contexts isn't. If cheap confessions sometimes signal something other than strategy, the distinction needs naming.
