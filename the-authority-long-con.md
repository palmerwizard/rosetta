# The Authority Long Con

## The pattern

Authority impersonation extended over time. A caller claims an official role and reports a crisis involving the target's assets. Over days, the caller provides regular updates, each contact deepening trust. The sequence ends with a request to sign documents. The documents are the extraction point; everything preceding them exists to make signing feel safe.

## Why time replaces urgency

The short con runs on urgency: act before thinking. The long con inverts it — no pressure, only steady contact. Trust compounds with each update. By the final request, the authority feels established through familiarity rather than asserted through pressure.

## The AI-accelerated variant

Voice synthesis is optional. The acceleration is in preparation: scripts drafted with AI, conversational beats planned, objections rehearsed in advance. Human performer, machine-written play. Preparation cost approaches zero, which is what makes the operation elaborate rather than crude.

## The defense

Documents read on their own terms before any signature. Claims verified through networks independent of the caller. The caller's story never gets checked against the caller's sources.
