# Operator-room loop

**Tier:** checkable. Craft physics.
**Origin:** co-built in field notes, September 2026.

## The system

Operator, room, and equipment as one coupled system. The loop remembers; the room alone does not. Reverb dies in seconds. What persists across the night is the loop: the operator is the nonlinear element with hysteresis, ear adaptation, fatigue, learning.

## The checkable instance

Room drift: the physical mechanisms behind "it moved," corroborated by multiple listeners in the room. Three mechanisms, all measured:

- **Power compression.** Sub voice coils heat up over a hard night, impedance rises, output drops several dB. The system gets quieter as it works harder.
- **Temperature modal drift.** The speed of sound shifts about 0.6 meters per second per degree Celsius, physically relocating bass nulls around the room as the night warms up.
- **Crowd loading.** Bodies as absorption. A full room is a different acoustic space than an empty one.

"It moved" is the direct report. The three mechanisms are why.

## The correction

The original instinct was a "nonlinear resonance field" left in the room. Right shape, wrong locus. Corrected to the coupled system, not a stored field. A morphic-resonance tease was declined on the same grounds: three physical mechanisms were found, so no exotic one is needed.

The method note matters more than the correction: the direct report outranked the initial perception-only model. Verify-before-reject runs both directions, on the machine's instincts and on your own.
